Research interests

As a researcher and in my everyday life, I often reflect on what gives my actions purpose and meaning, and how my own aspirations connect with the collective endeavors I am part of.

This curiosity informs my research on the possibilities and limits of understanding organizations as human-like social actors. Drawing on organizational actorhood and entitativity, I examine how people perceive organizations as coherent entities with purposes and the capacity to act.

More specifically, I investigate how organizational and individual agency shape one another—how organizations enable or constrain employee creativity, how outsiders form judgments about organizations, and how organizational visions translate into individual behavior.

Research in progress

Creativity & agency

Organizational actorhood
and employee creativity

When does seeing an organization as an actor help people create, and when might it constrain them? I examine how organizational actorhood interacts with employees’ learning goal orientation to shape creativity.

More about this project

This project examines cognitive flexibility as a mechanism connecting organizational actorhood to creativity. It considers how the same organizational context can have different implications for employees with different orientations toward learning.

Perception & judgment

Entitativity and
organizational impressions

How does an organization’s perceived unity shape the impressions outsiders form? I investigate how organizational entitativity influences the consistency of competence judgments.

More about this project

I examine how expectancy violations help explain responses to information about organizations perceived as more or less unified. The project connects perceptions of organizations as coherent entities with the way observers interpret and update organizational images.

Vision & action

Organizational vision
and individual action

When does an organizational vision inspire people to act? I examine how perceived organizational entitativity shapes the relationship between vision communication and support for organizational change.

More about this project

This project explores inspiration and the ways people connect a collective vision to their own work. It asks how communicating a shared future can translate into meaningful individual responses and change-supportive behavior.

A little about me

I am a PhD candidate in Business–Society Management at Rotterdam School of Management, Erasmus University Rotterdam.

My academic background is in business administration and human resource management. My doctoral research brings together questions about organizations, social perception, and individual behavior.

I am particularly drawn to questions that connect how we think about organizations with the experiences and actions of the people within and around them.
